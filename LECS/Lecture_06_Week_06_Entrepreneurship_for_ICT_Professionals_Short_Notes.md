# Lecture 06 — Week 06: Entrepreneurship for ICT Professionals

## Entrepreneurship and mindset

Entrepreneurship is the process of identifying an opportunity, mobilizing resources, accepting calculated risk, and creating value through a new or improved venture. Entrepreneurs show initiative, creativity, resilience, learning orientation, ethical judgment, and tolerance for uncertainty.

An **idea** becomes an **opportunity** when it solves a meaningful problem for a reachable market through a feasible and sustainable model.

## Entrepreneurial process

1. Observe problems, trends, and unmet needs.
2. Screen the opportunity: desirability, feasibility, viability, and timing.
3. Define customer segments and the value proposition.
4. Test critical assumptions with evidence.
5. Design the business/revenue model.
6. Acquire team, technology, finance, partners, and legal support.
7. Launch, measure, learn, adapt, and scale responsibly.

## ICT opportunities in Sri Lanka

Potential areas include software services, SaaS, fintech, health-tech, education technology, agriculture technology, tourism platforms, cybersecurity, AI/data solutions, logistics, BPO/KPO, and export-oriented digital products. Local problems can be tested locally and, where applicable, adapted for regional or global markets.

Enablers include technical talent, connectivity, diaspora links, incubators, universities, and global digital distribution. Challenges include finance, market size, policy uncertainty, payment access, skills gaps, talent retention, intellectual property, cybersecurity, and customer trust.

## Lean Startup

- **Build–Measure–Learn:** build the smallest useful experiment, measure real behavior, and learn whether assumptions hold.
- **MVP:** minimum viable product used to test the most important hypothesis; it is not simply a poor-quality final product.
- **Validated learning:** evidence that reduces business uncertainty.
- **Pivot:** a structured change in direction while retaining learning; **persevere** when evidence supports the model.

Useful metrics should be actionable and cohort-based; vanity metrics can look impressive without proving progress.

## Business model essentials

The Business Model Canvas considers customer segments, value propositions, channels, relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, partners, and cost structure. Common ICT revenue models include subscription, transaction fee, licensing, freemium, advertising, marketplace commission, and professional services.

## Finance, growth, and responsibility

Funding may come from bootstrapping, friends/family, grants, crowdfunding, angel investors, venture capital, bank finance, or strategic partners. Each differs in cost, risk, control, and expectations.

ICT founders should address privacy, security, accessibility, fairness, labor impacts, taxation, licensing, and intellectual property from the start. Growth without governance can magnify harm and operational risk.

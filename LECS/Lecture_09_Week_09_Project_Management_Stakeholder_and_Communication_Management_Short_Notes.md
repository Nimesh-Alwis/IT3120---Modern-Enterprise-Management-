# Lecture 09 — Week 09: Stakeholder and Communication Management

## Stakeholder management

A **stakeholder** is any person, group, or organization that can affect, be affected by, or perceive itself affected by a project. Early identification reduces surprise and improves requirements, support, and decisions.

### Main activities

1. Identify stakeholders and record relevant information in a stakeholder register.
2. Analyze power, interest, influence, impact, attitude, and expectations.
3. Plan suitable engagement strategies.
4. Manage engagement through relationships, participation, negotiation, and issue resolution.
5. Monitor changes and adjust strategies.

### Power–interest approach

- High power/high interest: manage closely.
- High power/low interest: keep satisfied.
- Low power/high interest: keep informed.
- Low power/low interest: monitor with proportionate effort.

Engagement may be classified as unaware, resistant, neutral, supportive, or leading. Compare current and desired states and plan how to close the gap.

## Communication management

Communication includes sender, encoding, message/channel, receiver, decoding, feedback, and noise. Effective communication is accurate, timely, relevant, understandable, accessible, and confirmed where necessary.

- **Interactive:** meetings, calls, workshops—fast feedback.
- **Push:** email, reports, notifications—sent to recipients but receipt does not prove understanding.
- **Pull:** portals, repositories, dashboards—users retrieve information when needed.

Communication channels for `n` people: **n(n − 1) / 2**. More team members sharply increase possible channels and coordination complexity.

## Communication management plan

Specify stakeholder information needs, content, frequency, format, owner, sender, recipients, channel, confidentiality, escalation, storage, language/accessibility, and feedback method. Sensitive data should follow least-privilege access and applicable policy/law.

## Meetings and conflict

Use a purpose, agenda, right participants, preparation, facilitation, time limits, documented decisions/actions, owners, and due dates. Resolve conflict by clarifying interests and evidence; collaboration/problem solving generally produces the most durable outcome, though other approaches may fit urgency or stakes.

## IT project application

Translate between technical and business language. Use prototypes/demos for shared understanding, define escalation paths for blockers, maintain decision and issue logs, and prevent distributed-team exclusion through deliberate asynchronous communication.

## Exam reminders

- Stakeholder engagement is continuous; a register alone is insufficient.
- Select channels based on urgency, complexity, sensitivity, audience, and need for feedback.
- Be able to calculate communication channels and explain its management implication.

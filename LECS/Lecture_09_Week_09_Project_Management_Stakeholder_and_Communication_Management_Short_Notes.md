# Lecture 09 — Week 09: Stakeholder and Communication Management

## Stakeholder management

A **stakeholder** is any person, group, or organization that can affect, be affected by, or perceive itself affected by a project. Early identification reduces surprise and improves requirements, support, and decisions.

### Main activities

1. Identify stakeholders and record relevant information in a stakeholder register.
2. Analyze power, interest, influence, impact, attitude, and expectations.
3. Plan suitable engagement strategies.
4. Manage engagement through relationships, participation, negotiation, and issue resolution.
5. Monitor changes and adjust strategies.

### Four stakeholder-management processes

| Process | Core work | Important outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Identify stakeholders | Find people/groups, interests, influence, impact, expectations | Stakeholder register, change/document updates |
| Plan stakeholder engagement | Choose strategies for current and desired engagement | Stakeholder engagement plan |
| Manage stakeholder engagement | Communicate, collaborate, negotiate, resolve issues, build support | Communications, issue/change records, lessons |
| Monitor stakeholder engagement | Reassess relationships and strategy effectiveness | Performance information and plan/document updates |

A stakeholder register commonly records identity/contact, role, expectations, interest, power/influence, impact, attitude, classification, communication needs, and confidentiality level. It should be reviewed whenever stakeholders or project conditions change.

### Power–interest approach

- High power/high interest: manage closely.
- High power/low interest: keep satisfied.
- Low power/high interest: keep informed.
- Low power/low interest: monitor with proportionate effort.

Engagement may be classified as unaware, resistant, neutral, supportive, or leading. Compare current and desired states and plan how to close the gap.

### Stakeholder analysis models

| Model | Dimensions | Best use |
|---|---|---|
| Power–Interest Grid | Authority × level of concern/involvement | General prioritization: manage closely, keep satisfied, keep informed, monitor |
| Power–Influence Grid | Formal power × ability to shape decisions/others | Organizational politics and decision pathways |
| Influence–Impact Grid | Ability to affect project × degree project affects them | Change management and impacted users |
| Salience Model | Power × legitimacy × urgency | Complex environments needing nuanced priority |

Salience categories include dormant (power only), discretionary (legitimacy only), demanding (urgency only), dominant (power + legitimacy), dangerous (power + urgency), dependent (legitimacy + urgency), and definitive (all three). Classification supports judgment; it must not be used to ignore low-power people heavily affected by the project.

## Communication management

Communication includes sender, encoding, message/channel, receiver, decoding, feedback, and noise. Effective communication is accurate, timely, relevant, understandable, accessible, and confirmed where necessary.

- **Interactive:** meetings, calls, workshops—fast feedback.
- **Push:** email, reports, notifications—sent to recipients but receipt does not prove understanding.
- **Pull:** portals, repositories, dashboards—users retrieve information when needed.

Communication channels for `n` people: **n(n − 1) / 2**. More team members sharply increase possible channels and coordination complexity.

### Three communication-management processes

1. **Plan communications:** analyze needs and define audience, content, owner, channel, format, frequency, storage, escalation, confidentiality, and feedback; output is the communications management plan.
2. **Manage communications:** create, collect, distribute, retrieve, store, and dispose of information; outputs include reports, minutes, presentations, dashboards, and other project communications.
3. **Monitor communications:** determine whether needs are being met, obtain feedback, identify gaps/barriers, and adjust plans/methods.

## Communication management plan

Specify stakeholder information needs, content, frequency, format, owner, sender, recipients, channel, confidentiality, escalation, storage, language/accessibility, and feedback method. Sensitive data should follow least-privilege access and applicable policy/law.

### Communication model and barriers

`Sender → encoding → message/channel → receiver → decoding → feedback`, with **noise** affecting any step. Barriers include distractions, technical jargon, language/cultural differences, assumptions, information overload, unsuitable channels, poor timing, and missing feedback.

The lecture's **5 Cs**:

- **Clear:** simple and unambiguous.
- **Concise:** focused without unnecessary detail.
- **Correct:** accurate, evidence-based, and suitable for the audience.
- **Coherent:** logically structured and internally consistent.
- **Controlled:** distributed only to authorized people through appropriate channels.

## Meetings and conflict

Use a purpose, agenda, right participants, preparation, facilitation, time limits, documented decisions/actions, owners, and due dates. Resolve conflict by clarifying interests and evidence; collaboration/problem solving generally produces the most durable outcome, though other approaches may fit urgency or stakes.

## IT project application

Translate between technical and business language. Use prototypes/demos for shared understanding, define escalation paths for blockers, maintain decision and issue logs, and prevent distributed-team exclusion through deliberate asynchronous communication.

### LMS case-study application

- Stakeholders: academic management/sponsor, faculty heads, lecturers, students, IT division, software vendor/developers, QA, and higher-education authorities.
- Example strategies: monthly sponsor reviews, lecturer requirement workshops, student surveys/pilot testing, weekly IT technical meetings, and vendor sprint reviews.
- Example plan: weekly progress report to management; daily development stand-up; monthly LMS demonstration; issue tracker for technical incidents.
- Use interactive meetings/demos for ambiguous requirements, push email/reports for targeted formal updates, and pull repositories/portals for manuals and large shared documentation.
- If lecturers resist an online-exam system while students are supportive, record lecturers as **resistant** and students as **supportive**; use hands-on demonstrations, training, pilot exams, support plans, and two-way workshops to reduce perceived risk.

## Exam reminders

- Stakeholder engagement is continuous; a register alone is insufficient.
- Select channels based on urgency, complexity, sensitivity, audience, and need for feedback.
- Be able to calculate communication channels and explain its management implication.
- Always match the stakeholder classification to an engagement action and match message complexity to an appropriate method.

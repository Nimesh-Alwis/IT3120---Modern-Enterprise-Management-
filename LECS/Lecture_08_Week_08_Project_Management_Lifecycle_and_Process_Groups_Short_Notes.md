# Lecture 08 — Week 08: Project Management Lifecycle and Process Groups

## Project fundamentals

A **project** is a temporary effort undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result. Operations are ongoing and repetitive. Project management applies knowledge, skills, tools, and techniques to satisfy objectives and stakeholder expectations.

Constraints are interconnected: scope, schedule, cost, quality, resources, and risk. A change to one often affects the others.

Key project characteristics are **temporary**, **unique output**, **progressive elaboration**, and a **value-delivery focus**. Progressive elaboration means plans/details become more precise as knowledge increases; it is controlled learning, not uncontrolled scope expansion.

Project management combines knowledge/frameworks, human skills, tools/techniques, value focus, systems thinking, and tailoring to size, complexity, risk, industry, organization, and delivery approach.

## Project and product lifecycles

- **Project lifecycle:** phases from initiation to closure.
- **Product lifecycle:** the broader life of the resulting product, often continuing after the project.
- **Predictive/Waterfall:** scope planned early; suitable where requirements are stable and change is costly.
- **Adaptive/Agile:** iterative and incremental; suitable where uncertainty and feedback are high.
- **Hybrid:** deliberately combines predictive and adaptive practices.

Lifecycle characteristics:

- Phases may be sequential or overlap and each should have defined objectives/deliverables.
- Phase gates review performance and decide to continue, revise, pause, or stop.
- Uncertainty and stakeholder influence are normally highest early.
- Cost/resources usually increase during execution and fall near closure.
- Cost of change generally increases later because completed work may need rework.
- A typical IT lifecycle can include requirements → design → development → testing → deployment → maintenance. This technical lifecycle is related to, but not identical with, management process groups.

## Five process groups

1. **Initiating:** establish purpose, high-level scope, feasibility, sponsor authority, and key stakeholders; produce the charter.
2. **Planning:** define scope and create integrated plans for schedule, cost, quality, resources, communications, risk, procurement, and stakeholders.
3. **Executing:** coordinate people and resources to produce deliverables and manage engagement.
4. **Monitoring and Controlling:** compare actual with plan, validate/control scope, manage changes, risks, issues, quality, schedule, and cost.
5. **Closing:** obtain acceptance, transition deliverables, close contracts, release resources, archive records, and capture lessons learned.

Process groups can overlap and repeat; they are not automatically the same as project phases.

## Governance and key artifacts

- **Business case:** why the investment is worthwhile.
- **Project charter:** formally authorizes the project and project manager.
- **Project management plan:** integrated basis for execution and control.
- **Baselines:** approved versions of scope, schedule, and cost used for comparison.
- **Change request/change log:** controlled record of proposed and decided changes.
- **Issue, risk, and lessons registers:** support active management and organizational learning.

## PMBOK 7 perspective

PMBOK 7 emphasizes value delivery, principles, performance domains, tailoring, systems thinking, leadership, stakeholder engagement, quality, complexity, risk, adaptability, and change. Tailoring selects an approach suited to context rather than applying every practice mechanically.

### Twelve project-management principles

1. **Stewardship:** act responsibly, ethically, and accountably; manage resources with care.
2. **Collaborative team:** create trust, respect, ownership, and open communication.
3. **Stakeholder engagement:** identify and involve stakeholders throughout.
4. **Value focus:** connect outputs to benefits and organizational purpose.
5. **Systems thinking:** recognize interactions and downstream effects.
6. **Leadership:** demonstrate direction, service, motivation, judgment, and emotional intelligence.
7. **Tailoring:** adapt methods and artifacts to context.
8. **Quality:** build quality into processes and deliverables.
9. **Complexity:** recognize and navigate human, technical, and organizational complexity.
10. **Risk:** optimize responses to threats and opportunities continuously.
11. **Adaptability and resilience:** adjust and recover from change/disruption.
12. **Enable change:** help people and organizations reach the intended future state.

### Lifecycle versus process groups

| Project lifecycle | Process groups |
|---|---|
| High-level phases describing **when/where** work exists over time | Logical management activities describing **how** work is managed |
| Varies by project and delivery approach | Initiating, planning, executing, monitoring/controlling, closing |
| A phase can contain several process groups | Groups can overlap, repeat, and operate within multiple phases |

Process-group interaction is iterative: planning guides execution; execution produces data/deliverables; monitoring compares performance and triggers correction/change; learning updates plans; formal acceptance enables closure.

### Process-group outputs to remember

- Initiating: charter, high-level objectives/scope, sponsor authority, stakeholder identification.
- Planning: integrated plan and subsidiary plans; scope/schedule/cost baselines; risk, quality, resource, communication, procurement, and engagement plans.
- Executing: deliverables, team coordination, communications, procurements, quality work, engagement, and issue handling.
- Monitoring/controlling: performance information/forecasts, verified results, approved/rejected change requests, plan/document updates.
- Closing: formal acceptance/transition, final report, contract/admin closure, released resources, archived knowledge and lessons learned.

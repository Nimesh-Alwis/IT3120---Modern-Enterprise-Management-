# Lecture 03 — Week 03: Introduction to Economics

## Foundations

Economics studies how scarce resources are allocated among competing wants.

- **Scarcity** forces choices.
- **Opportunity cost** is the value of the best alternative forgone.
- **Microeconomics** examines individuals, firms, and markets.
- **Macroeconomics** examines economy-wide output, inflation, unemployment, growth, and policy.

## Demand and supply

- The **law of demand**: other things equal, quantity demanded falls when price rises.
- Demand shifts with income, tastes, expectations, population, and prices of substitutes or complements.
- The **law of supply**: other things equal, quantity supplied rises when price rises.
- Supply shifts with input costs, technology, taxes/subsidies, expectations, and number of sellers.
- **Equilibrium** occurs where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. A shortage puts upward pressure on price; a surplus puts downward pressure on price.

A price change causes movement **along** a curve; a non-price factor shifts the entire curve.

## Elasticity

Price elasticity of demand measures responsiveness: percentage change in quantity demanded divided by percentage change in price.

- Elastic demand: absolute elasticity greater than 1.
- Inelastic demand: absolute elasticity less than 1.
- Unit elastic: absolute elasticity equals 1.

Availability of substitutes, necessity, budget share, and time affect elasticity. SaaS firms use elasticity when testing subscriptions and discounts.

## Production and costs

- **Fixed costs** do not change with short-run output.
- **Variable costs** change with output.
- **Total cost = fixed cost + variable cost.**
- **Average cost = total cost / quantity.**
- **Marginal cost** is the additional cost of one more unit.
- **Sunk costs** have already occurred and should not drive future choices.

Digital products often have high initial development cost and low marginal replication cost. Economies of scale reduce average cost as output grows; diseconomies can appear when complexity and coordination increase.

## Market structures

- **Perfect competition:** many sellers and homogeneous products.
- **Monopolistic competition:** many differentiated sellers.
- **Oligopoly:** a few interdependent firms.
- **Monopoly:** a single dominant supplier with strong entry barriers.

Network effects, data advantages, switching costs, standards, and intellectual property influence competition in IT markets.

## Macroeconomics

- **GDP:** value of final goods and services produced within a country.
- **Inflation:** sustained increase in the general price level.
- **Unemployment:** people willing and able to work but unable to find work.
- **Fiscal policy:** government spending and taxation.
- **Monetary policy:** money supply and interest-rate actions by the central bank.
- Exchange rates, inflation, interest rates, and growth affect IT wages, imports, exports, investment, and project budgets.

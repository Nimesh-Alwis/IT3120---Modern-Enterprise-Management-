# Lecture 04 — Week 04: Introduction to Human Resource Management

## HRM purpose

Human Resource Management attracts, develops, motivates, evaluates, rewards, and retains people so employee capability supports organizational strategy.

## Workforce planning, recruitment, and selection

1. Forecast workforce demand and available supply.
2. Analyze each job and prepare job descriptions/specifications.
3. Recruit internally or externally.
4. Screen applications and assess candidates consistently.
5. Select, offer, onboard, and review results.

Recruitment builds a pool; selection chooses the best fit. Valid selection can combine structured interviews, work samples, tests, and reference checks. Decisions should be job-related, fair, documented, and free of discrimination.

## Performance management

Performance management is a continuous cycle of goal setting, coaching, review, development, and recognition—not merely an annual appraisal.

- Use specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound (**SMART**) goals.
- Combine results with behaviors and competencies.
- Common rating errors include halo/horns effects, recency, leniency, central tendency, and personal bias.
- Constructive feedback should be timely, evidence-based, specific, and improvement-oriented.

## Rewards

- **Extrinsic:** salary, incentives, benefits, promotion, and formal recognition.
- **Intrinsic:** achievement, autonomy, mastery, purpose, and meaningful responsibility.
- Reward systems require internal equity, external competitiveness, affordability, transparency, and alignment with desired behavior.

## Motivation and personality

- **Maslow:** needs progress from basic/psychological security toward belonging, esteem, and self-actualization.
- **Herzberg:** hygiene factors reduce dissatisfaction; motivators create satisfaction and growth.
- **Expectancy theory:** effort increases when employees expect it to improve performance and lead to valued rewards.
- **Equity theory:** employees compare their input–outcome balance with others.
- Personality affects communication and work preferences, but labels should not replace observation or fair assessment.

## Leadership, power, and negotiation

Leadership styles include directive/autocratic, participative/democratic, delegative, transactional, transformational, and situational approaches. Effective leaders adapt to task, risk, competence, and team needs.

Negotiation should distinguish positions from interests, prepare alternatives (**BATNA**), seek objective criteria, generate options, and document agreements. Power may come from formal authority, reward, coercion, expertise, or relationships.

## Project teams

Teams commonly move through forming, storming, norming, performing, and adjourning. High-performing IT teams need clear roles, psychological safety, complementary skills, communication norms, accountability, conflict resolution, and shared goals.

## Exam reminders

- Separate recruitment, selection, appraisal, and performance management.
- Be ready to apply motivation theories to workplace scenarios.
- Explain why a leadership style may fit one situation but fail in another.

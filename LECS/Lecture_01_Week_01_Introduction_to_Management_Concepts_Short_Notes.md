# Lecture 01 — Week 01: Introduction to Management Concepts

## Learning focus

- Understand organizations and the functions of management.
- Distinguish levels, skills, and roles of managers.
- Compare major management approaches and organizational structures.
- Explain power and employee motivation.

## Organization and management

An **organization** is a deliberately structured group of people who combine resources and follow agreed processes to achieve shared goals. Its basic elements are people, goals, structure, resources, rules/processes, and leadership.

**Management** is the coordinated process of using people and other resources to achieve organizational goals efficiently and effectively.

- **Efficiency:** doing work with minimum waste of time, money, and resources.
- **Effectiveness:** achieving the intended objectives.

## Four management functions

1. **Planning** — define goals, forecast conditions, and select actions.
2. **Organizing** — divide work, allocate resources, and establish authority.
3. **Leading** — communicate, influence, motivate, and guide employees.
4. **Controlling** — set standards, measure results, compare results with standards, and take corrective action.

## Managers

- **Top managers:** strategy and organization-wide direction.
- **Middle managers:** translate strategy into departmental plans.
- **First-line managers:** supervise day-to-day operational work.
- Core skills are **technical**, **human/interpersonal**, and **conceptual** skills.
- Mintzberg groups managerial roles into **interpersonal**, **informational**, and **decisional** roles.

## Management approaches

- **Classical:** productivity, structure, specialization, and formal authority; includes scientific and administrative management.
- **Behavioral:** human needs, motivation, groups, and leadership.
- **Modern:** systems thinking, contingency thinking, data, quality, and continuous adaptation.
- An organization is an **open system**: it obtains inputs from its environment, transforms them, and returns outputs while using feedback.

## Organizational structures

- **Functional:** people grouped by specialist function; efficient but may create silos.
- **Divisional:** grouped by product, customer, or geography; responsive but can duplicate resources.
- **Matrix:** employees report across functional and project lines; flexible but may cause authority conflict.
- **Tall vs. flat:** tall structures have more levels and narrow spans; flat structures have fewer levels and wider spans.
- **Centralization vs. decentralization:** decisions concentrated at the top versus distributed closer to operations.

## Power and motivation

Power bases include **legitimate**, **reward**, **coercive**, **expert**, and **referent** power. Sustainable leadership relies more on credibility, expertise, fairness, and influence than coercion.

Motivation links individual needs with organizational goals. Managers can improve it through meaningful work, clear goals, feedback, recognition, fair rewards, participation, development, and supportive leadership.

## Exam reminders

- Do not confuse efficiency with effectiveness.
- Learn the four management functions in sequence and the control feedback loop.
- Be able to classify a manager's action as a Mintzberg role, skill, or management function.
- Compare structural alternatives by both advantage and limitation.

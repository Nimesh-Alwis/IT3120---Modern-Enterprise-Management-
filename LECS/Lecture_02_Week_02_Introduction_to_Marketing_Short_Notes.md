# Lecture 02 — Week 02: Introduction to Marketing

## Core idea

Marketing identifies customer needs, creates and communicates value, delivers an offering, and maintains profitable customer relationships. It is broader than advertising or selling.

### Marketing versus selling

| Marketing | Selling |
|---|---|
| Begins with customer needs and market insight | Begins with an existing product |
| Long-term strategy and relationship focus | Short-term transaction focus |
| Includes research, STP, product, price, channels, communication, experience, and CRM | Primarily persuades a customer to purchase |
| Creates value and then captures value in return | Converts the offering into immediate revenue |

Technology does not sell itself: even a technically strong product can fail through weak problem–market fit, positioning, customer experience, or adoption.

### Evolution of marketing

1. **Product-centric era:** production efficiency and product availability.
2. **Customer-centric era:** needs and satisfaction.
3. **Values-driven era:** emotional connection, social purpose, and trust.
4. **Digital era:** online engagement and two-way interaction.
5. **Data and AI era:** analytics, prediction, automation, and personalization.
6. **Marketing 6.0:** human-centered, technology-enabled, immersive **phygital** experiences connecting physical and digital touchpoints.

## Key concepts

- **Needs** are basic human requirements; **wants** are shaped by culture and personality; **demands** are wants backed by buying ability.
- **Value proposition:** the benefit and experience promised to a target customer.
- **Customer value:** perceived benefits compared with monetary, time, effort, and risk costs.
- **Satisfaction:** the comparison between expected and experienced performance.

## Marketing process

1. Analyze the market and customer needs.
2. Segment the market.
3. Select attractive target segments.
4. Decide a differentiated position and value proposition.
5. Design the marketing mix.
6. Implement, measure, learn, and improve.

**STP** means segmentation, targeting, and positioning. Segments may be demographic, geographic, psychographic, or behavioral. A useful segment is measurable, accessible, substantial, differentiable, and actionable.

### Strategy hierarchy

- **Vision:** desired long-term future.
- **Marketing objectives:** measurable results that support the vision.
- **Strategy:** broad route to value and competitive advantage.
- **Tactics/campaigns:** specific audiences, content, channels, budgets, and timing.
- **Metrics and learning:** compare results with objectives and adjust.

### STP in detail

- **Segmentation:** divide a broad market into meaningful groups using demographics, location, behavior, needs, usage, lifestyle, or firm characteristics.
- **Targeting:** assess segment size, growth, accessibility, competitive intensity, fit, and profitability; choose whom to serve.
- **Positioning:** establish a distinctive place in the target customer's mind relative to alternatives.
- A useful positioning statement identifies the target, need/category, main benefit, and credible reason to believe.
- The **Customer Value Proposition (CVP)** clearly explains why the chosen customer should prefer the offering; it must remain consistent across customer touchpoints.

## Marketing mix

- **Product:** features, quality, design, brand, service, and product lifecycle.
- **Price:** list price, discounts, payment model, and perceived value.
- **Place:** distribution channels and customer access.
- **Promotion:** advertising, public relations, sales promotion, personal selling, content, and digital communication.

For services, the extended mix adds **People, Process, and Physical Evidence**.

## Research and product management

- **Primary research:** surveys, interviews, observation, experiments, and focus groups.
- **Secondary research:** existing internal and external data.
- Good research defines the problem, gathers suitable data, analyzes it objectively, and turns findings into decisions.
- Product management connects customer problems, business goals, and technical delivery. Common concerns include product-market fit, roadmap priorities, lifecycle stage, and user feedback.

### Market-research workflow

1. Define the decision problem and research objectives.
2. Select primary and/or secondary data and a suitable sample.
3. Collect data ethically through surveys, interviews, focus groups, observation, experiments, web analytics, social insights, or existing reports/databases.
4. Clean, analyze, and interpret patterns; distinguish correlation from causation.
5. Report actionable findings, limitations, and recommendations.

AI can analyze large datasets, sentiment, patterns, and forecasts quickly, but output can inherit biased data, privacy problems, weak samples, or incorrect assumptions.

### Product management and lifecycle

Product decisions balance three tests: **do customers need it, can technology build it, and does it make business sense?** Product managers use discovery, requirements, prioritization, roadmaps, prototypes/MVPs, Agile iterations, usage data, and feedback.

| Lifecycle stage | Typical condition | Marketing/product priority |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Low awareness, high launch cost | Educate market, validate use, improve product |
| Growth | Rapid adoption and new competitors | Scale, differentiate, expand channels/features |
| Maturity | Slower growth and saturation | Retain, improve efficiency, extend/reposition |
| Decline | Falling demand or substitution | Harvest, niche, redesign, replace, or discontinue |

IT products such as SaaS, mobile apps, and platforms require continuous updates, usability, integration, reliability, cybersecurity, and support rather than a one-time physical-product mindset.

## Digital marketing and metrics

Digital channels include search, social media, email, content, mobile, display, and affiliates. Useful metrics include reach, impressions, click-through rate, conversion rate, customer acquisition cost, retention, churn, and customer lifetime value.

- **CTR = clicks / impressions × 100**
- **Conversion rate = desired actions / relevant visitors or leads × 100**
- **CAC = acquisition spending / new customers acquired**
- **ROI = (return − investment cost) / investment cost × 100**
- Select metrics that connect to the campaign goal; likes and impressions can be vanity metrics if they do not lead to meaningful behavior.

Marketing 6.0 uses IT for data collection/analytics, automation and chatbots, personalization, integration across web/mobile/store channels, AR/VR or immersive experiences, and real-time optimization.

## Brand and CRM

- A **brand** is the set of meanings and expectations associated with an offering; brand equity can increase trust, loyalty, and willingness to pay.
- **CRM** uses people, processes, and technology to acquire, serve, retain, and grow customers.
- Customer data must be accurate, purpose-limited, secure, and used ethically with appropriate consent.

### Brand, CRM, and customer experience

- **Brand identity:** the intended name, design, values, personality, and message.
- **Brand image:** how customers actually perceive the brand.
- **Brand equity:** added value created through awareness, associations, perceived quality, trust, experience, and loyalty.
- **Experience-driven branding:** manages every touchpoint to create a memorable, consistent emotional connection.
- CRM objectives include retention, loyalty, personalized service, customer lifetime value, and coordinated marketing/sales/service.
- CRM systems store profiles and interaction history, support lead/pipeline management, automate communication and service, and generate analytics. Lecture examples: **Salesforce** (enterprise scale/AI), **HubSpot** (integrated, user-friendly inbound tools), and **Zoho CRM** (cost-effective and customizable for SMEs).
- **Customer Experience (CX)** is the customer's total perception across awareness, evaluation, purchase, use, support, and renewal. A seamless omnichannel journey should preserve context and consistency.

## Exam reminders

- Selling is one activity inside marketing, not a synonym for it.
- Apply STP before selecting the marketing mix.
- Link every digital metric to a business objective; high traffic alone does not prove success.
- In scenarios, follow the chain: research → STP → CVP/positioning → mix → digital execution → metrics → CRM/CX improvement.
